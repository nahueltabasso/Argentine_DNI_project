== *Executive Summary*

This work builds an *end-to-end pipeline* to detect Argentine ID Cards (DNI) using a *YOLOv26 OBB* model.

To do this work I used a public images downloaded from the internet, some pictures taken by me and some pictures taken by my friends. As this samples are not enough to train a model, I increased the number of samples using synthetic data generated from templates and fake data with the library of `faker`. The final dataset has aproximately 900 samples. This dataset are not public, except for synthetic examples.

The *YOLOv26 OBB small* model was selected for production, reaching *mAP@50 = 0.956*
with *Precision 0.97 / Recall 0.98 / F1 0.98*. Since inference runs on a server
(no edge/latency constraint), the small model is preferred over the nano variant,
which stayed ~4.6 pp below in mAP@50.

== *Introduction & Objective*

=== *Problem Statement*

Detect and localize the Argentine ID card in images (rotated, over different backgrounds).

=== *Success Criteria*

* Detect both document sides: `front` and `back`.
* Detect the document in arbitrary rotations and backgrounds.
* Achieve a mAP@50 >= 90 and mAP@95 >= 85 on a held-out test set of real images.

=== *Scope*

* *In scope:* card detection (2 classes `front` and `back`) with OBB.
* *Out of scope:* OCR, face detection, MRZ reading, etc.

== *Background: Why Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBB)?*

Standard axis-aligned boxes enclose a rotated card with a large amount of
background, hurting localization quality. OBB predicts a box aligned to the real
orientation of the card, which is critical for cropping and perspective correction.

* Standard box: `cls x_center y_center w h`
* *OBB box* (this project): `cls x1 y1 x2 y2 x3 y3 x4 y4`

== *Process Overview*

The dataset construction and model training were split into a sequential pipeline
of six notebooks. Each notebook produces the input for the next one, following the
naming convention `NN_<step>_vX.Y.Z.ipynb`.

[cols="1,3,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| # | Notebook | Goal | Output

| 00
| `00_draw_DNI_zones`
| Delimit and draw the field zones (photo, names, MRZ, etc.) over the ID-card
templates and store their relative coordinates.
| `config/coords_data.json` with the normalized zones per document type
(DNI/Card, front/back).

| 01
| `01_generate_sinthetic_examples`
| Generate synthetic ID cards by filling the templates with fake personal data
(`faker`) and pasting real faces (Flickr-Faces-HQ) into the photo zone.
| Synthetic front/back ID-card images.

| 02
| `02_backgrounds`
| Generate realistic backgrounds (desks, tables, hands, floors) using image
generation models (`gpt-image-1` and Gemini) to place the cards on.
| Background image bank (`backgrounds/`, `backgrounds_1024/`).

| 03
| `03_generate_DNIs_sinthetic_ds`
| Compose each synthetic card over a random background applying random rotation,
perspective warp, drop shadow.
| Synthetic dataset with images + YOLO **OBB** labels (`cls x1 y1 x2 y2 x3 y3 x4 y4`).

| 04
| `04_annotate_DNIs_ds`
| Auto-label the *real* samples (public + own photos) using **SAM3** segmentation
to produce OBB annotations without manual drawing (pre-labeling).
| Pre-labeled real samples in YOLO OBB format.

| 05
| `05_review_and_correct_labels`
| Quality control: inspect the pre-labeled dataset in **FiftyOne** and fix the
wrong boxes in **CVAT**.
| Curated and corrected ground-truth dataset.

| 06
| `06_train_and_metrics`
| Fine-tune the **YOLOv26 OBB** model (nano and small) and evaluate it on the
held-out `test` split (Precision, Recall, F1, mAP@50).
| Fine-tuned weights (`yolo26s-obb-DNI-det.pt`) and evaluation report.
|===

=== Data strategy

Because real annotated samples were scarce, the dataset combines two sources:

* *Synthetic data* (notebooks 00–03): fully controlled generation where the labels
are exact by construction (no manual annotation needed).
* *Real data* (notebooks 04–05): a smaller set of real photos, pre-labeled with
SAM3 and manually corrected, which grounds the model on real-world appearance.

This mix reaches ~900 samples and lets the detector learn arbitrary rotations and
backgrounds while keeping annotation cost low.

== *Model Training*

=== *Training configuration*

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Parameter | Value

| Base checkpoints | `yolo26n-obb`, `yolo26s-obb`
| Epochs | 100
| Image size | 640
| Batch | 16
| Patience | 25
| Seed | 42 (fixed, fair comparison)
| Device | CUDA (GPU) / CPU fallback
|===

=== *Evaluation protocol*

Metrics are computed *only on the test view* to avoid counting train/val ground
truths as false negatives.

[cols="2,1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Parameter | Value | Role

| `conf` | 0.6 | Min confidence to keep a detection (inference)
| `iou` (NMS) | 0.3 | Suppress overlapping duplicate boxes (inference)
| `iou` (eval) | 0.5 | Min overlap to count a prediction as TP (evaluation)
|===

=== *Model Selection & decision*

We select the *smallest* model whose gap to the best `mAP@50` stays within *1 pp*.
The nano model is ~4.6 pp below, i.e. *outside* the tolerance. In addition, inference
runs on a *server* (size/latency are not hard constraints). Therefore the *small*
model is kept as the production checkpoint (`yolo26s-obb-DNI-det.pt`).

== *Error Analysis*

* Residual errors concentrate on `front` class, typically when the card is very rotated.

== *Risks and Threats to Validity*

* Domain Gap:* Heavy reliance on synthetic data may not reflect actual camera usage.
* Small Test Set:* Differences of a few points may fall within the margin of error.
* Selection Bias:* Backgrounds generated by image models may not always match real-world usage.

== *Future Work*

. Increase the number of samples with *real* ID-Card photos.
. Increase the number of samples in our *test* set.
. Run robustness stress tests (blur, JPEG, low light, motion).
. Periodic re-training / recalibration as new data arrives.

== *Conclusions*

The pipeline successfully produces a working OBB detector from a mostly synthetic dataset. The *small* model meets the success criterion (*mAP@50 = 0.956 >= 0.90*)
with balanced precision/recall and is selected for server-side deployment. The main open risk is the *synthetic-to-real domain gap*, addressed in Future Work.
